# Tessarium

*cryp-TESS-er-a* — from **crypto** + **tessera**, a single tile in a mosaic.
The `t` is shared. One address is one tile; a seed phrase decides which mosaic
you are looking at.

Three BIP-39 words plus a number address every ~3 m square on Earth — under a
mapping that is **private to your seed phrase**.

```
dream.tourist.creek.2703        # under one seed
pair.social.april.9605          # the same point, under another
```

Two people with different seed phrases hold completely unrelated maps of the
same planet. An address is meaningless to anyone who does not have your phrase.

## How it works

**Grid.** The globe is divided into 6,553,600 latitude rows, grouped into 4096
bands. Each band has its own column count, tracking cos(latitude) so cells stay
near-square instead of collapsing at the poles. 55.7 trillion cells, ~3.05 m
tall and 2.88–3.00 m wide. All arithmetic is in integer nanodegrees; no
floating point appears anywhere in the encode path, so a browser and a server
agree exactly.

**Permutation.** The cell index is encrypted with an FE1 generalized Feistel
network over Z_a × Z_b, where a = 2¹⁸·25 and b = 2¹⁹·25. Their product is
exactly 2048³·10⁴, so the permutation is a bijection on the address space with
no cycle-walking. Ten rounds, HMAC-SHA256 as the round function, keyed from
your seed phrase.

**Addresses.** Mixed radix (2048, 2048, 2048, 10000) over the permuted index.

## Two design choices worth knowing about

**Addresses are scattered, not hierarchical.** Neighbouring squares get
unrelated addresses. This costs the ability to truncate for coarser precision —
a genuinely nice feature — and buys a large privacy margin: a leaked address
exposes one 3 m square rather than the 10,000 squares in an enclosing block.
For a system whose premise is a private map, leak blast radius wins.

**About 35% of word combinations decode to nothing.** The grid uses 65% of the
address space, so a mistyped address is far more likely to be rejected outright
than to resolve somewhere plausible. Combined with scattering, a one-word typo
that does decode lands hundreds of kilometres away, where it is obviously
wrong. Measured: of 1,289 single-word typos that decoded at all, none landed
within 100 km.

## Seed phrases

**24 words only.** 12-word phrases carry 128 bits of entropy, which Grover's
algorithm reduces to an effective 64. 24 words leaves 128 bits standing. The
construction is otherwise entirely hash-based and needs no post-quantum
migration — nothing here rests on factoring or discrete log.

**Generate a fresh phrase. Do not reuse a wallet seed.** Anyone who learns a
few (address, true location) pairs is doing cryptanalysis against your key. If
that key also holds funds, you have combined two unrelated risks for no reason.

## Scaffolding

`reference/` and `js/` are temporary and **outranked by F\***. They exist
because the F\* toolchain was not available when the design was being
validated, and something had to establish that the band table arithmetic
actually closed. It didn't, twice — see the corrections in
`roadmap-progress.md`.

They contradict the architecture. The whole point of extracting server, C and
WASM targets from one proved source is that no platform reimplements the
algorithm by hand, because hand-written copies drift and the drift is silent.
Two hand-written copies currently exist.

The sharper problem is `vectors/vectors.json`. It is generated by
`reference/gen_vectors.py`, and every future implementation is checked against
it, which makes the Python the de facto source of truth for the entire system.
That is backwards, and it is the thing to fix first.

What happens to them:

| Path | Fate |
|---|---|
| `design/grid_design.py` | **Stays.** Offline, runs once, uses floating-point trigonometry deliberately, emits a data table rather than shipping code. This is why it is separate from the integer-only runtime. |
| `reference/` | **Deleted** once the F\* spec can execute as the oracle. |
| `reference/gen_vectors.py` | **Deleted.** Vectors get regenerated from the F\* spec; `vectors.json` itself survives unchanged. |
| `js/` | Open question — delete when WASM lands, or keep deliberately as an independent cross-check? Recorded in `roadmap.md`. |

The target is the HACL\* pattern: the F\* specification *is* the readable
reference, executed via the normalizer or extracted, with no separate
hand-written oracle to drift from it.

Until then, treat anything in these two directories as scaffolding rather than
architecture, and do not add features to them.

## Trust boundary

Verification, once it exists, will cover the grid arithmetic, the Feistel
bijection, and the codec. It will not cover:

- **The F\* extraction pipeline and `ocamlopt`** — trusted, not proved.
- **Whether the mapping is unguessable.** The strongest honest claim is
  "provably reversible for any round function, and unpredictable assuming
  HMAC-SHA256 behaves as a PRF." That assumption is standard and reasonable,
  but it is an assumption.
- **Whether the grid is well designed.** Proof establishes that it is
  consistent, not that it is sensible.

## Name

Nearest neighbour worth knowing about: the **Cryptomeria cipher** (C2), a
proprietary 10-round Feistel used for DRM on SD cards and DVD-Audio. Similar
name, and — awkwardly — also a 10-round Feistel. No relation, and nothing
shared beyond the round count.

## Prior art

[Open Location Code](https://github.com/google/open-location-code) (Plus Codes,
Apache-2.0) solves the public, unkeyed case well and is the right choice if you
do not need a private mapping. The differentiator here is the per-seed map.

This project is a clean-room design. It does not use what3words' wordlist,
grid, or algorithm, and contributors should not consult them.
